# Start here

Assurance Console checks your books against the documents that are supposed to agree with them, and says, for every entry that does not agree, **why** in plain words. It also keeps a register of every export invoice and what is still missing from it. It runs entirely on your own computer: nothing is uploaded, nothing is filed, and it never logs in to any portal.

It has two parts, shown as the two tabs in the maroon band at the top of every page:

| Part | What it is for | What you give it | What you get back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reconciliation** | Checking two sets of figures that should agree | GST: your Tally purchase register and the GSTR-2B from the GST portal (and, optionally, your sales register and filed GSTR-1). Bank: the bank statement and your Tally bank book. A party: their statement of account and your ledger for them | A verdict in one sentence, the working that ties the two sides, and every open entry with the reason it is open and what to do. Excel, PDF and CSV reports, letters to suppliers, a balance confirmation letter |
| **Exports** | Keeping every export invoice's papers, payment, refund and eBRC in one register per exporter | Shipping bills, bills of lading, invoices, GSTR-1, bank advices, DGFT and ICEGATE downloads, your bank's EDPMS statement, your own shipping bill register | A line per invoice showing what has arrived and what has not, deadlines worked out under the right rules, refunds and drawback not received with the ICEGATE code, and drafted letters and worksheets to fix what is wrong |

## Who it is for

It is written for the business owner as much as for the accountant. Every screen uses plain words ("Not in your books", "Paid, not yet through the bank") instead of accounting shorthand, and every open entry explains itself. An accountant gets the detail in the downloads: the input tax credit working, a bank reconciliation statement, a party bridge, sheet after sheet of schedules.

## What it promises, and what it does not

+ Everything stays on this computer. There is no account and no cloud, it works without an internet connection, and none of your data is ever sent anywhere. The one thing it does online is look for updates.
+ Every entry left open says why: what it is, what was checked, the closest thing found on the other side and why it was not paired, what it means for you, and what to do.
+ The working always adds up, or says by how much it does not and that a figure was probably read wrongly.
+ Nothing is matched against your money silently in Exports: a payment is only counted against an invoice when a document names the invoice, or when you confirm it or choose the invoices yourself.
+ Every file you give it is kept, named for what it is, and can be opened, saved, shared or found in its folder later.
x It does not file returns, submit anything to the GST portal, ICEGATE, DGFT or a bank, or log in to any of them. It reads the files you download from them.
x It does not replace your judgement. Letters and worksheets are drafts; figures read from a scan must be spot-checked; a suggested match must be confirmed by you.
x It is not an accounting package. It never changes your Tally data; it tells you what to change.
x It is for one person on one computer. There is no sharing between computers, no user accounts and no automatic backup.

## How to read this guide

Each part explains one area in the order you would meet it, with pictures taken from the app running on its made-up sample company, **Kesari Textile Mills**. Throughout:

+ a tick marks something the app **can** do,
x a cross marks something it **cannot** do, or does only partly.

> A box like this marks a limit worth stopping on - usually something that could mislead you if you did not know it.

Every limit is said where you would meet it, and they are all gathered again, with the capabilities, in the two chapters near the end: **Everything it can do** and **Everything it cannot do**. The last pages hold troubleshooting, a glossary, and a table of every number the app uses (tolerances, windows, caps and limits).

This guide describes **version 2.3.5**. Where the app's behaviour depends on a setting, the default is given and the setting is named.
